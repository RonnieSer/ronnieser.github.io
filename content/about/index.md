I’m a master’s student in geology at The University of Texas at El Paso, working on ways to use machine learning to help map the Moon’s south pole.

For my thesis, I’m exploring how machine learning can help make geologic mapping more consistent and efficient. I use lunar images and terrain data, along with existing geologic maps, to train and compare models that identify different geologic units. This project has given me the chance to combine my interest in planetary geology with new skills in ArcGIS and Python. I enjoy working between the maps and the data, and thinking about what the patterns we see can tell us about the Moon’s geologic history.

Beyond my current research, I love being in the field and seeing how rocks and structures record the history of a place. My interest in field geology has made me curious about the processes that shape landscapes on Earth and across the solar system. I’m especially interested in planetary geology, icy worlds, structural geology, geodynamics, and meteorite impacts. I hope to pursue a PhD that allows me to explore one or more of these interests and continue learning about the processes that shape planetary bodies.

My past work has given me opportunities to approach geological questions from different angles. I’ve mapped faulted and folded rocks in the California desert, helped conduct seismic, resistivity, and radar surveys in New Mexico, and made a geologic map for a local land trust. Each experience has taught me to look closely at the evidence in front of me and consider how it fits into a larger story.

Thanks for stopping by! Have a look around for my research and a few stories from the field.
